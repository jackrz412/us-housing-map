Mortgage stress, county by county: where households are stretched now

Mortgage defaults usually need two things at once: a household that is stretched (a heavy payment burden or thin equity) and a shock such as a job loss. These layers show where the first half is loaded, and where delinquency is already rising. The Index layers combine them into two scores against fixed 2019 national benchmarks. They describe conditions; they don't predict defaults. A backtest found the scores did not anticipate how delinquency changed from 2019 to 2025. Notes

Grey hatching means Data not available: the source doesn't publish that county, or its sample is too small (fewer than 30 loans or owner households). Missing data is excluded, not estimated.

Colors use fixed breaks, so a color means the same thing in every county. Four layers use two-sided scales with a grey middle: delinquency is compared with each county's own 2017–2019 average, owner cost burden with the US share, and the two index scores with their 2019 national benchmark (1.0). Blue means below that midpoint, not "safe".

The index scores are descriptive: 1.0 means a county's inputs sit at the 2019 national level. They are not probabilities of default, and the weights are a stated choice, not fitted to outcomes. Index methodology and backtest.
